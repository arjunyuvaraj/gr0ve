## 1. School Overview

### School Hours

- School day: **8:00 AM – 4:10 PM**
- Athletes on late-bus days may stay until **6:30 PM**
- Classes are approximately **50 minutes** each
- **Wednesday:** modified "Projects" day — the final period is reserved for clubs, internships, and senior experience

### Schedule Structure

BCA uses a **trimester system** (3 trimesters per year). The period structure is:

| Period | Type | Notes |
|--------|------|-------|
| Periods 1–8 | Core and full-year elective classes | Scheduled M/T/R/F (non-Wednesday) |
| Period 9 | Trimester electives, sports, theatre | M/R or T/F (2 days/week) or M/T/R/F (4 days/week) |
| Wednesday | Projects day / Senior Experience | Distinct from M/T/R/F schedule |

### Days Notation

| Notation | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| M/R | Monday / Thursday (2 days/week) |
| T/F | Tuesday / Friday (2 days/week) |
| M/R and T/F | 4 days/week within a trimester |
| M/T/R/F | All non-Wednesday days (4 days/week) |

### Unscheduled Period Limits *(not including lunch)*

- Rising 10th graders: max **7** unscheduled periods/week
- Rising 11th graders: max **8** unscheduled periods/week
- Students with too many unscheduled periods may be assigned electives to fill gaps

### Wednesday / Projects Day

- Dedicated to clubs, internship experiences, and senior research projects
- **Seniors:** Full Wednesdays spent in supervised Senior Experience internships/research
- Most clubs meet on Wednesday afternoons

### Campus Policies

- **Closed campus** — students remain on campus from arrival until dismissal
- Free periods built into schedules for homework and independent study
- Library and labs available during open/free periods
- Chromebooks issued per Acceptable Use Policy; district dress code applies
- No food or drink in computer labs or during instruction

### Library Availability

- Open during all school hours and during students' free periods
- Study tables, research databases, and library staff available
- Chromebook repairs and tech support referrals available at front desk

### Clubs Overview

- Dozens of clubs spanning academics, arts, service, and special interests
- Most clubs meet once per week (Wednesdays or weekday mornings) under a faculty sponsor
- New clubs can be formed with a student proposal and a faculty sponsor
- Club fair held each fall semester for sign-ups
- Example clubs: DECA, HOSA, Math Club, Model UN, Robotics, Red Cross, Wellness Club, Girls Who Code, Best Buddies, FFA (for Agriscience students)

### Cafeteria / Lunch

- Managed by **Chartwells**; menus available on the BCTS nutrition site
- Students use their school ID to purchase meals
- Free/reduced lunch: apply online through the district portal
- Students may bring lunch from home

### Technology Policies

- All students receive a Chromebook governed by the **Acceptable Use Policy (AUP)**
- Personal devices are permitted but must not interfere with learning
- Chromebook issues: submit a District Tech Helpdesk ticket or visit library tech staff
- Printing available in library; color printing may be limited

### Academy Directory

| ACAHA | Academy of Culinary Arts & Hospitality Administration |

> AVPA is further divided by sub-track: **AVPA-V** (Visual Arts), **AVPA-M** (Music), and **AVPA-T** (Theatre).

### How Academy Affects Your Schooling

Your academy determines nearly every aspect of your BCA experience:

**Core Course Sequence:** Each academy has a specific, required sequence of core science, math, and specialty courses. For example, AMST students take Anatomy & Physiology as freshmen, while AEDT students take Physics I and Chemistry in 9th–10th grade.

**Science Requirements:** The science pathway varies significantly. AAST students take AP Chemistry as their 11th grade science; AMST students take AP Biology; other academies take Honors Biology or IB Biology SL.

**Elective Access:** Some electives are restricted by academy. For example, the ABF-only Fed Challenge elective requires ABF enrollment. Bioethics is not available to AMST students. Intro to Makerspace is not available to AEDT students (covered in their core curriculum).

**IB Pathways:** Different academies have different IB Diploma options available. Beginning 2026–27, IB Diploma is available to ALL academies. ACAHA students already have IB Business SL built into their core.

**Specialty Courses:** AVPA students have access to acting, dance, music theory, studio art, and performing arts courses not open to other academies. ATCS students have a dedicated computer science core sequence. ACAHA students take culinary arts, hotel management, and hospitality courses.

### Art Credit Rules

- **6 art credits** required for graduation
- **3 credits** are fulfilled automatically by the mandatory 9th grade art class
- **3 more credits** still needed — can be earned by:
  - Taking 3 one-trimester art electives (each worth 1 credit)
  - Taking 1 full-year 2-day art elective (worth 2 credits)
  - Taking Lunch Choir or another qualifying extracurricular art class
- Art-eligible courses are flagged throughout this database

### Minimum Credit Totals

- Students typically earn 30–35+ credits per year
- Students who fall significantly behind in credits should meet with their counselor immediately to discuss a remediation plan
- Credits from outside courses (e.g., online courses, community college dual enrollment) may apply — see your counselor

## 5. Volunteer Hours & Service Requirements

### Does BCA Require Volunteer Hours?

BCA does not mandate a specific number of volunteer hours as a standalone graduation requirement. However, **IB Diploma candidates** must complete **CAS (Creativity, Activity, Service)** requirements across 11th and 12th grade, which includes documented service activity.

For non-IB students, volunteer hours are encouraged and may be logged for scholarship applications and personal records, but are not required for graduation from BCA itself.

### CAS Requirements (IB Diploma Students Only)

IB Diploma candidates must demonstrate:

| CAS Strand | Description | Examples |
|------------|-------------|---------|
| Creativity | Engaging in creative arts or design | Music, visual art, writing, drama |
| Activity | Physical activity and healthy living | Sports, dance, fitness, hiking |
| Service | Unpaid, meaningful community benefit | Tutoring, food banks, hospital volunteering |

- CAS must be documented with **goals, reflections, and evidence**
- A **CAS Project** (collaborative, sustained) is required — usually in 12th grade
- Minimum: engagement across both 11th and 12th grades with regular reflection
- CAS is reviewed by your IB coordinator; incomplete CAS means no IB Diploma

### Qualifying Service Examples (for CAS or General Records)

- Tutoring or mentoring younger students
- Hospital or clinic volunteering
- Community food pantries or soup kitchens
- Environmental cleanup projects
- Animal shelter assistance
- Fundraising for nonprofit organizations
- Library or community center volunteering
- FFA community service projects (for Agriscience students)

### Step-by-Step Guide: Logging and Documenting Service

1. **Before volunteering:** Confirm the organization is legitimate and non-profit/community-based
2. **During service:** Keep track of hours, dates, and supervisor contact information
3. **After each session:** Write a brief reflection (especially important for CAS students)
4. **Get documentation:** Ask your supervisor to sign a log sheet or provide a letter on organization letterhead
5. **Submit to:** Your school counselor (for general records) or your IB coordinator (for CAS)
6. **Deadlines:** CAS portfolios are reviewed at the end of each school year; submit documentation before final exams

### Who Approves Hours?

- **General volunteer hours:** School counselor
- **CAS credit (IB students):** IB Coordinator at BCA
- **IB Supervisor:** Faculty advisor or IB Coordinator assigned to each student

## 7. Scheduling System

### Trimester & Period Structure

- BCA operates on **3 trimesters** per year
- **Periods 1–8** host full-year and semester-length courses (run M/T/R/F)
- **Period 9** hosts trimester electives, sports, and theatre (run M/R or T/F, sometimes M/T/R/F)
- **Wednesday** is "Projects Day" — used for clubs, senior experience, and special programs

### How Schedules Are Built

- BCA makes best effort to honor all requests — nearly all are accommodated
- **Priority order:** 12th graders → 11th graders → lower grades
- **For each student:** core classes first → 3/4-day electives → 2-day electives → period 9
- 11th grade requests cover **periods 1–8 only**; period 9 is handled separately in June
- Students may submit **one alternate elective choice** in case their first is unavailable

### Scheduling Timelines

#### Rising 10th Grade

| Step | When |
|------|------|
| Submit course requests via Google Form | Feb 11 – Mar 6 |
| Receive scheduled course list | Mid June |
| Request period 9 electives for T1 + Add/Drop window | Late June |
| Schedule changes with counselor | Early September |

#### Rising 11th Grade

| Step | When |
|------|------|
| Scheduling meeting with counselor | January |
| Meet with counselors; enter requests (periods 1–8 only) | Jan 29 – Mar 6 |
| Receive course listing; request period 9 for T1 | June |

#### Rising 12th Grade

| Step | When |
|------|------|
| Meet with counselor; enter requests | January – early March |
| BCA emails scheduled courses | June |

### Schedule Change Instructions (Step-by-Step)

1. **Identify the issue:** Know which course you want to add, drop, or swap before meeting with your counselor
2. **Check the window:** Schedule changes are processed during designated Add/Drop periods (typically late June and early September)
3. **Email or visit your counselor:** Explain the change you're requesting and why
4. **Check prerequisites:** Confirm you meet any prerequisites for a new course you want to add
5. **Confirm the change:** Your counselor will confirm whether the change is possible given space, timing, and requirements
6. **Receive updated schedule:** Changes appear in your student portal once processed
7. **Dropping an AP or IB course:** See FAQ — there are specific rules and timelines for this

> **Important:** After the Add/Drop window closes, schedule changes are very limited and require counselor approval with valid academic reason.

## 8. IB Diploma Program

> **Beginning 2026–27, the IB Diploma is available to ALL academies.** Previously it was primarily offered through ABF and ACAHA.

### What Is the IB Diploma?

The International Baccalaureate (IB) Diploma Programme is a rigorous, internationally recognized two-year program (11th and 12th grade) that can yield college credit and demonstrates advanced academic achievement. Students who do not pursue the full diploma may still take individual IB courses for the associated prestige and potential college credit.

### IB Diploma Core Requirements

#### 1. Six IB Courses (3 or 4 at Higher Level)

| Course Group | Requirement |
|-------------|------------|
| English | HL (IB Literature HL or IB Literature & Language HL) |
| Mathematics | IB Math SL or HL (12th grade) |
| History | IB History SL (1 year) or HL (2 years) |
| World Language | IB French/Spanish SL or HL; OR Mandarin Ab Initio SL |
| Science | IB Biology SL, IB Environmental Systems SL, or IB CS SL |
| Elective | Another IB course (varies by academy) |

- Must have **at least 3 HL courses** (some pathways require 4)
- HL = Higher Level (more rigorous, more content, more time)
- SL = Standard Level

#### 2. Theory of Knowledge (TOK)

- Full-year, 2-day/week course
- Must take in **both 11th and 12th grades**
- Can be scheduled in periods 1–8 or at period 9 to preserve a 2-day elective slot
- Culminates in a 1,600-word essay and an exhibition of 3 original objects
- Covers how we know what we know across mathematics, sciences, arts, history, and human sciences

#### 3. CAS (Creativity, Activity, Service)

- Ongoing across 11th and 12th grade
- Must document creative pursuits, physical activity, and community service
- Requires goals, regular reflection entries, and a collaborative CAS project
- Reviewed by IB coordinator; failure to complete CAS = no IB Diploma

#### 4. Extended Essay (EE)

- Independent, self-directed research paper
- Focused question in a subject of the student's choice
- Must be approximately **4,000 words**
- Completed across junior and senior years under faculty supervision
- Intro to EE via the **IB Research Seminar** (trimester elective, 11th grade)

### Should I Pursue the IB Diploma? (Decision Guide)

Ask yourself these questions:

| Question | If YES | If NO |
|---------|--------|-------|
| Are you planning 4 years of world language? | ✅ Good fit | ❌ Likely not a fit |
| Are you planning an advanced history course? | ✅ Good fit | ❌ Likely not a fit |
| Will you be in Calculus as an 11th grader? | ✅ Good fit | ⚠️ May not satisfy IB Math |
| Are you comfortable with rigorous writing and exams? | ✅ Good fit | ⚠️ Consider individual IB courses instead |
| Do you want maximum elective flexibility? | ⚠️ Diploma limits electives | ✅ Individual courses better |

> **Trade-off:** The IB Diploma limits elective flexibility. You may not be able to take AP Physics, AP Art History, Adv Math Topics, Data Structures, and similar courses if pursuing the full diploma.

## 9. Research Programs at BCA

Research is one of the most distinctive and frequently misunderstood aspects of BCA. Many students want to do research but don't know how to get in, what it entails, or what the pathway looks like. This section explains everything.

### What Is Research at BCA?

BCA offers **independent research programs** across multiple departments. These are not typical classes — research is a trimester elective (typically meeting twice a week) where a student works with a faculty mentor on an original, self-directed scientific or engineering project. Research students may present at science fairs, publish papers, or enter academic competitions.

Research is highly competitive for some tracks and open-entry for others. Read carefully.

---

### Research Tracks Overview

| Research Program | Department | Open To | Entry Point |
|-----------------|-----------|---------|------------|
| Cell Biology Research | Biology | All (except AMST via different path) | Research in Cell Biology elective (or Experimental Bio for AMST) |
| Chemistry/Nanotechnology Research | Chemistry | Gr 10–12 | Foundations of Nano elective |
| Engineering Research | Engineering | Gr 10–12 (AEDT priority) | Conversation with engineering teacher |
| Agriscience Research | Biology/Agriculture | Gr 9–12 | Intro to Agriscience → Agricultural Research Methods |
| Social Science Research | Social Studies | All | Available as trimester elective |
| Intro to STEM Research | Cross-disciplinary | All | Available as trimester elective (any trimester) |
| AMST Experimental Biology | Biology | AMST only | Core course — automatic in 9th grade |

---

### Cell Biology / Molecular Biology Research

**Who can do it?**
- Any student **except** AMST students (who have their own path via Experimental Biology)
- Grades 9–12

**What is the prerequisite?**
- You must first complete the **Research in Cell Biology** trimester elective
- This is NOT available to AMST students
- Research in Cell Biology does **NOT** count as a science, biology, or medical elective — it is a standalone prereq

**What happens in Research in Cell Biology?**
- Introduction to primary scientific literature
- How to use literature to develop a research project proposal
- Basics of working with cultured cells and molecular analysis techniques
- Upon completion, you are eligible to enroll in independent research with a mentor

**What is the actual research elective?**
- Titled **~Cell Biology Research**
- Trimester-long elective (2 days/week; scheduled on an individual basis)
- Students design and conduct experiments, collect and analyze data, present findings at fairs, competitions, or potentially publish to a professional journal
- Research meets twice per week but students should expect **additional time in the lab outside of class**
- Students work with a faculty mentor of their choice

**AMST students:** Must first complete at least one trimester of **Experimental Biology** (9th grade core course) before becoming eligible for Cell Biology Research

---

### Chemistry / Nanotechnology Research

**Who can do it?**
- Grades 10–12
- All academies

**What is the prerequisite?**
- Complete the **Foundations of Nano** trimester elective first
- This elective introduces the fundamental theories and experimental practices of nanoscience

**What is the actual research elective?**
- Titled **~Chemistry Research**
- Students perform original experiments under supervisor/mentor guidance
- Use state-of-the-art scientific equipment
- Participate in science competitions
- Assessed on participation and research progress

---

### Engineering Research

**Who can do it?**
- Grades 10–12
- Priority given to **AEDT students** who will be juniors (though students from other academies with experience are considered)
- New rising sophomores with relevant experience may also apply

**How do you enter?**
- Have a **conversation with one of the engineering teachers prior to signing up** — this is required
- Students typically join an **ongoing project**, but new projects are considered
- Applied research — may involve collaboration with professionals in the field (e.g., a researcher working on coral restoration)

**What resources are available?**
- Electronics lab and electronic fabrication
- Mechanical engineering lab and mechanical fabrication
- 3D printers
- Makerspace facilities

---

### Agriscience Research

**Who can do it?**
- Grades 9–12

**Two-step entry pathway:**

| Step | Course | Notes |
|------|--------|-------|
| Step 1 | **Intro to Agriscience** (trimester elective) | Gateway course; must take first |
| Step 2 | **Agricultural Research Methods** (trimester elective) | Identifies topic, prepares lab notebook |
| Step 3 | **~Agriscience Research** (trimester elective, ongoing) | Conduct the actual research |

**What is Agriscience Research?**
- Takes place in the Environmental Science Center and greenhouse
- Uses scientific principles and emerging technologies to solve complex problems in agriculture, food, and natural resources
- Projects may involve plants, food, biofuels, marine organisms, and sustainable practices
- Membership in **BCA FFA** is required to compete at the NJ State FFA Agriscience Fair

---

### AMST Experimental Biology (AMST Students Only)

**What is it?**
- A 9th grade **core course** for AMST students (not an elective)
- Introduces experimental design, basic lab skills, and data collection in biological sciences
- Students rotate through each biology research teacher across the year (one trimester each)
- Topics: project development, experimental design, primary literature, bioinformatics, technical writing, lab techniques, data collection, data analysis, and data presentation

**How does this connect to future research?**
- After completing at least **one trimester of Experimental Biology**, AMST students become eligible to enroll in **Cell Biology Research** as an elective
- This replaces the Research in Cell Biology prereq that non-AMST students must take

---

### Social Science Research

- Available as a trimester elective (M/R) across multiple trimesters
- Open to all students and academies
- Good entry point for students interested in psychology, economics, or social sciences
- No listed prerequisite

---

### Intro to STEM Research

- Available as a trimester elective (T/F in T1, T/F in T2, M/R and T/F in T3)
- Open to all students and academies
- A broad introduction to the research process across STEM disciplines
- Good first step for students who are curious about research but unsure which track to pursue

### Key Research Rules and Tips

- Research electives typically meet **2 days per week** but require significant additional time in the lab
- Research does **NOT** automatically satisfy a science credit — check with your counselor
- Research in Cell Biology does **NOT** count as a science, biology, or medical elective
- AMST students follow a different research pathway — do NOT sign up for Research in Cell Biology
- Interest in a research program? Talk to the relevant teacher early — ideally in the spring before the year you want to start
- Some mentors have limited spots — apply early and be prepared to describe your interests

## 15. Senior Experience

### What Is Senior Experience?

Senior Experience is a **mandatory graduation requirement** for all BCA seniors. It is a supervised internship, research placement, or creative project that takes place every **Wednesday** throughout the senior year.

### Key Details

| Item | Details |
|------|---------|
| When | Every Wednesday — full-day |
| Required? | YES — graduation requirement |
| Credits Awarded | **9 academic credits** |
| Who selects placement? | Collaborative process with counselors and mentors |
| Setting | External professional sites (hospitals, labs, businesses, studios, etc.) |

### How Placements Work

1. During junior year (spring), students begin discussing Senior Experience interests with their counselor
2. BCA coordinates with professional mentors and organizations
3. Students are placed with a mentor relevant to their academy and academic interests
4. Examples: science lab research, hospital shadowing, culinary internship, engineering firm, arts studio, financial institution
5. Students work under professional supervision every Wednesday

### Expectations

- Attend every Wednesday session; absences must be reported
- Maintain professional conduct at the placement site
- Complete any required reflections, logs, or projects associated with the experience
- Some placements require background checks or health clearances in advance

### Reporting Absences

- All Senior Experience absences must be called in to the **attendance line: (201) 343-6000 ext. 2226**
- Same day — do not wait
- Excessive unexcused absences may jeopardize graduation requirements

## 16. Frequently Asked Questions

---

### Scheduling Questions

**How do I change my schedule?**
Contact your school counselor during the Add/Drop window (late June or early September). Bring a clear reason for the change. Outside of these windows, changes require counselor approval and are generally only allowed for academic necessity. See Section 7 for full step-by-step instructions.

**When can I request my electives?**
Elective requests are submitted through a Google Form during the spring (Feb–Mar) for periods 1–8. Period 9 trimester electives are requested separately in late June. Each trimester, there is a new opportunity to request a different elective for the next trimester.

**What happens if my requested course is full?**
BCA will attempt to place you in your alternate choice. If that is also unavailable, your counselor will work with you to find a suitable alternative.

**Can I take more than one trimester elective at a time?**
No. Period 9 runs as a single block — you have one trimester elective at a time.

**Can I have a free period instead of an elective?**
Only up to the unscheduled period limits: rising 10th graders may have up to 7 unscheduled periods per week; rising 11th graders may have up to 8. Exceeding this leads to automatic elective placement.

---

### AP and IB Questions

**Can I drop an AP class?**
Dropping an AP class after the Add/Drop window closes requires counselor approval and a valid academic reason. Dropping very late (e.g., after the October deadline) may result in a W (withdrawal) on your transcript, which some colleges notice. Discuss with your counselor before dropping.

**Can I drop an IB course?**
IB courses (especially HL two-year sequences) have significant consequences if dropped mid-year or late. For two-year HL commitments (History, World Language), dropping in 12th grade is particularly complicated. Meet with your counselor as early as possible.

**Do I have to take the AP or IB exam?**
Yes. All students enrolled in AP or IB courses are required to sit for the exam. Exam fees apply. If cost is a concern, speak with your counselor about fee waiver options.

**Can I take an AP class without taking the AP exam?**
No. Exam participation is mandatory for all enrolled AP/IB students.

**What is the difference between IB SL and HL?**
HL (Higher Level) is more rigorous, covers more content, meets more frequently, and has harder assessments. HL courses also carry more potential college credit. SL (Standard Level) is still challenging but less intensive than HL.

---

### Research Questions

**How do I get into research?**
The pathway depends on your academy and the type of research. Most students need to complete a prerequisite elective first (e.g., Research in Cell Biology for molecular biology research; Foundations of Nano for chemistry research). See Section 9 for full pathways by research type.

**I'm in AMST — can I do research?**
Yes! AMST students have their own pathway: complete Experimental Biology (your 9th grade core course) first. After at least one trimester of Experimental Biology, you are eligible to enroll in Cell Biology Research. You do NOT take Research in Cell Biology — that is for non-AMST students.

**Does Research in Cell Biology count as a science credit?**
No. Research in Cell Biology does NOT count as a science, biology, or medical elective. It is a prerequisite elective that unlocks independent research.

**Does independent research count as a science credit?**
Not automatically. Check with your counselor — this depends on your academy's credit structure.

**Can I start research in 9th grade?**
Generally not for independent research, but some Agriscience Research students begin the pathway in 9th grade (starting with Intro Agriscience). AMST students begin Experimental Biology as a 9th grade core course. Other research tracks typically start in 10th or 11th grade.

**What competitions can I enter if I do research?**
Science fairs (Bergen County, NJ Regional, ISEF/Regeneron), Regeneron STS, NJ State FFA Agriscience Fair (requires FFA membership), math competitions, and more. Your research mentor can guide you.

---

### Art Credit Questions

**How many art credits do I need to graduate?**
6 art credits total. 3 are covered by your mandatory 9th grade art class. You need 3 more, earned through art-eligible trimester electives, a full-year visual/performing arts elective, or Lunch Choir.

**Which electives count for art credit?**
All electives marked "Art Credit: YES" in the elective tables in this database count. Examples include Ceramics, Choir, Concert Band, Orchestra, Musical Theater, Video Production, History of Rock Music, Design for 3D Printing, Digital Photography, Creative Art Workshop, and more.

**Does Intro to ASL count as an art credit?**
No. ASL courses do not count toward the art credit requirement and do not count toward your GPA.

---

### Course Eligibility Questions

**Can I repeat electives?**
Most electives are not repeatable. Notable exceptions: Ceramics can be taken again only through Add/Drop (not during regular course request). History of Rock Music can be taken across trimesters because each trimester covers a different era. Interactive Design has multiple levels (I, II, III, Advanced I, II, III) that can be taken sequentially. Always check with your counselor.

**Can I take courses outside BCA?**
Potentially. Some students take courses at community colleges or online. Credits from outside BCA may or may not apply toward graduation requirements. Always consult your counselor BEFORE enrolling in outside courses.

**Can I take Foundations of Computer Science if I'm not ATCS?**
Yes! Foundations of Computer Science (FoCS) is specifically designed for non-ATCS students. It is available as a full-year 2-day elective or as individual trimester electives. Completing all 3 trimesters of FoCS unlocks AP CS, IB CS, and most CS trimester electives.

**I'm ATCS — can I take FoCS?**
No. ATCS students have their own core computer science sequence (Intro to CS, AP CS A Data Structures, etc.). FoCS is not available to ATCS students.

**Can any student take culinary electives?**
Yes — the Culinary Design trimester electives (Baking Fundamentals, Western Cuisine, etc.) are open to all students as period 9 electives. The ACAHA culinary core sequence is restricted to ACAHA students.

---

### Transcripts and Records

**How do I request a transcript?**
Contact your school counselor or the main office. Official transcripts are typically requested through your student portal or a form provided by the counseling office. For college applications, use the Common App or Coalition App transcript request system linked to BCA.

**How long does a transcript take?**
Allow at least 5–7 business days for standard requests; longer during peak college application season (October–January). Request early.

**Can I see my unofficial transcript?**
Yes — access through your student portal or ask your counselor.

---

### Academic Concerns

**What happens if I fail a class?**
Failing a core required course may require summer school or retaking the course. Meet with your counselor immediately. Failing an AP or IB course has no automatic penalty beyond the grade itself, but it may affect college acceptances if grades drop significantly.

**What if I'm struggling in an AP or IB class?**
Talk to your teacher first. Then speak with your counselor. Options include tutoring (peer tutoring programs exist at BCA), extra help sessions, and in rare cases, dropping the course during the appropriate window.

**Can I audit a class without taking the exam?**
No. All AP/IB students must take the exam. If you want to take a rigorous course without the exam pressure, look into honors-level options.

---

### Senior Questions

**What is Senior Experience and is it required?**
Yes — it is a required graduation component worth 9 academic credits. All seniors spend every Wednesday in a supervised internship or research setting. See Section 15 for full details.

**When do seniors pick their Senior Experience placement?**
Placement discussions begin in the spring of junior year. Work with your counselor to identify interests and get matched.

**Does Financial Literacy count as a graduation requirement?**
Yes, for most academies (AAST, AEDT, AMST, ATCS, AVPA). It is an online, asynchronous, self-paced course taken in 12th grade. ABF and ACAHA students have business/economics courses that fulfill equivalent requirements.
